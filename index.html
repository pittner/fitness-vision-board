12 questions before you choose your fitness goals

Answer them alone, with a notebook, in 15 minutes. Be honest, nobody else reads them. The answers that surprise you point to the goals that will actually stick.

  1. What kind of movement makes me feel most alive?
  2. When was the last time I felt truly strong, and what was I doing?
  3. What stopped my last fitness streak, and what would prevent it this time?
  4. Am I training for how I look, how I feel or what I can do?
  5. If I could only do one type of exercise for a year, which would it be?
  6. What fitness goal have I been putting off because it feels too hard?
  7. Do I train better alone or with other people?
  8. What does my body need more of right now: strength, flexibility or rest?
  9. How many hours a week can I realistically give to training?
  10. What injury or pain am I ignoring that I should address first?
  11. If I reach my fitness goal by December 2027, how will my daily life be different?
  12. Who in my life would notice the change, and what would they say?

Plan your fitness year in 45 minutes

  1. 10 minutes: honest audit. Write down what you did last week, how your body feels now, and what you have been avoiding.
  2. 15 minutes: answer the questions. Go through the 12 questions below. Short, honest answers; nobody else reads them.

Questions

What should go on a fitness vision board?

Your goal in your own words, a first step for this week, and the reason it matters to you. This page has 36 fitness goals in 6 groups (strength and gym, running and cardio, flexibility and recovery, outdoor and adventure, sports and teams, fitness habits and mindset), each with a first step and a free lock screen maker.

Does a fitness vision board actually work?

A board does not lift the weight for you, but seeing your goal every day keeps it present when you decide how to spend your evening or what to eat. The habit that works best is putting it on your phone lock screen so you cannot ignore it.

How many fitness goals should I set for 2027?

Three is enough: one strength or cardio goal, one habit goal and one recovery goal. Fewer goals get done more often than long lists.

Is this different from a weight loss vision board?

Yes. Weight loss focuses on a number on the scale. A fitness vision board focuses on what your body can do: run further, lift more, move better, recover faster. The mindset shift matters.
